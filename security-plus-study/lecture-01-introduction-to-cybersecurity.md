## 2. What Is Cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting computers, networks, applications, systems and information from unauthorised access, misuse, damage or disruption.

Its purpose is to protect digital assets and ensure that information remains confidential, accurate and available when needed.

Examples of digital assets include:

- Personal information
- Bank account information
- Business records
- Computer systems and networks
- Passwords and login credentials

## 3. The CIA Triad

The CIA Triad is one of the fundamental models in information security.

It consists of three principles:

### Confidentiality

Ensures that information is accessible only to authorised people.

**Examples:**
- Password protection
- Encryption
- Access permissions

### Integrity

Ensures that information remains accurate, complete and has not been improperly changed.

**Examples:**
- Hashing
- Digital signatures
- File integrity monitoring

### Availability

Ensures that systems and information are accessible when authorised users need them.

**Examples:**
- Backups
- Redundant systems
- System maintenance and recovery planning

**Key lesson:** Effective cybersecurity must protect confidentiality, integrity and availability together.

## 4. AAA: Authentication, Authorization and Accounting

AAA is a framework used to manage and monitor access to systems.

| Principle | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Authentication | Verifies who you are | Password or multifactor authentication |
| Authorization | Determines what you can access | Permission to view a particular file |
| Accounting | Records activities and actions | Login and access logs |

Remember:

- Authentication: Who are you?
- Authorization: What are you allowed to do?
- Accounting: What did you do?

## 5. Security Controls

Security controls are safeguards used to reduce security risks and protect information systems.

### A. Categories of Security Controls

| Category | Explanation | Example |
|---|---|---|
| Administrative | Policies, procedures and rules | Security awareness training |
| Technical | Technology-based safeguards | Firewalls and encryption |
| Physical | Protect physical facilities and equipment | Locks and security cameras |

### B. Functions of Security Controls

- **Preventive:** Stops or reduces the chance of an incident.
- **Detective:** Identifies suspicious activity or incidents.
- **Corrective:** Helps restore systems or correct problems.
- **Deterrent:** Discourages unwanted behaviour.
- **Compensating:** Provides an alternative safeguard when the preferred control cannot be used.
- **Directive:** Gives instructions or establishes required behaviour.

A single security control can serve more than one purpose.

## 6. Threats, Vulnerabilities and Risk

These three terms are related, but they do not mean the same thing.

**Threat:** Something capable of causing harm to a system or organisation.

Example: A phishing attacker attempting to steal login credentials.

**Vulnerability:** A weakness that could be exploited.

Example: An account that does not have adequate access protection.

**Risk:** The possibility of harm when a threat takes advantage of a vulnerability, considered in terms of likelihood and impact.

Example: An attacker exploiting weak account protection could gain unauthorised access to sensitive information.

**Remember:**
- Threat = potential source of harm.
- Vulnerability = weakness.
- Risk = potential consequence of the threat exploiting the weakness.

## 7. Practical Scenario: A Phishing Email

Imagine an employee receives an email pretending to come from the organisation's IT department. The email asks the employee to provide their login credentials.

The employee provides the credentials, allowing an unauthorised person to access the account.

Security implications:

- **Confidentiality:** Private information could be exposed.
- **Integrity:** Information could be changed without permission.
- **Availability:** The legitimate user could lose access to the account.

Possible security controls include multifactor authentication, email filtering, security awareness training and login monitoring.

## 10. Key Takeaways

This lecture introduced the foundations of cybersecurity:
